# Shot-first direction

Read when spatial/camera intent is underspecified. Choose the actors' route and camera beats before building proxies. From a location sheet retain only relevant ground height, wall position/height, corridor width, obstacle placement and silhouettes visible during this shot. State any scale assumption. The reference's complete city is not the set.

Use sparse anchors to express the user's action. C3 smoothing is not physical support: a floor-to-wall turn must follow real faces, stay on their common edge during a supported transfer, or be an explicitly intended source-backed jump. Evaluate the entire transition, including the first roll frame. Never round a corner through empty space just because the path is smooth.

Plan speed contrast with a shared action clock and separate camera motion. Keep both actors accounted for in every beat; intentional offscreen intervals need a reason. A 90° wall-floor roll is selected by the actual projected normal, not the sign of an Euler angle. Pair it with a readable wall surface and enough depth cues to understand the new floor.

Brief, held composition can be a deliberate tracking shot; long static/relative holds need explicit intent. Choose framing and speed constraints appropriate to the user's style. Avoid blanket loose bands that make any result pass. The spec is creative intent, the numerical preflight its measurable contract, and the video still needs human artistic acceptance.
